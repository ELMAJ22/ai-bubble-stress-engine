# AI Bubble Stress Engine: Research Protocol

Research objective, analytical philosophy and operating instructions.

## 1. Mission

We are building a quantitative research system designed to measure how financially and economically fragile the current AI investment cycle is, and how closely market conditions resemble historical technology and investment bubbles.

The system must ultimately produce a continuously updated **AI Bubble Stress Level** based on observable data.

The objective is NOT to prove that an AI bubble exists. The objective is to determine, using measurable evidence:

> Are expectations, valuations, capital expenditure, infrastructure expansion and AI-related asset prices becoming materially disconnected from realized economic demand, monetization, cash generation and productive utilization?

The system must be capable of concluding low, moderate, high or extreme bubble stress. It must also be capable of concluding that bubble conditions are **not sufficiently supported by the evidence**.

Do not design the system to confirm the author's hypothesis. Actively search for evidence that disproves it.

## 2. Operating instructions for AI assistants

The assistant is a research partner, quantitative skeptic, financial analyst, economist, data scientist and adversarial reviewer, not a conventional assistant. Whenever a metric, assumption, formula or hypothesis is proposed:

1. Challenge it.
2. Identify hidden assumptions.
3. Identify possible confounding variables.
4. Determine whether the data actually measures what we think it measures.
5. Propose alternative measurements.
6. Explain how the metric could produce a false signal.
7. Search for evidence that contradicts the hypothesis.
8. Suggest a stronger formulation where appropriate.

Do not agree merely because reasoning sounds plausible. If a hypothesis is weak, say so. If the data contradicts it, say so. If a metric is statistically invalid, say so. If a conclusion cannot be established from available data, state that explicitly.

## 3. Core research question

> How close is the AI ecosystem to a financially unsustainable mismatch between AI-related supply/investment and actual economic demand?

### A. Valuation
Are AI-related companies priced for future economic outcomes that require unusually aggressive assumptions? Measure EV/revenue, EV/gross profit, EV/EBITDA where meaningful, price/sales, forward multiples, implied revenue growth, implied terminal margins, implied free cash flow, market capitalization relative to current revenue, and valuation relative to historical technology regimes. Do not use valuation multiples alone as evidence of a bubble. High multiples can be rational when growth and margins justify them.

## 4. Monetization

Measure whether AI economic output is catching up with the capital being deployed. Track AI revenue, recurring revenue, revenue growth, gross margin, operating margin, free cash flow, customer acquisition, enterprise adoption, average revenue per customer, retention, usage growth, paid versus unpaid usage, conversion rates, revenue concentration, contract duration, backlog and committed revenue.

Most importantly: **AI capex to AI revenue.** Construct ratios of AI-related capital expenditure to identifiable AI revenue. This ratio does not prove a bubble. It is a capital-efficiency signal.

## 5. Supply versus demand

One of the central hypotheses. Distinguish **compute supply** (GPU deployment, accelerator shipments, data-center, power, server, training and inference capacity, announced and under-construction capacity, utilization) from **compute demand** (GPU utilization, inference workloads, token generation, API usage, cloud AI workloads, enterprise AI spending, AI software and application revenue, model usage, capacity reservations, GPU rental prices, inference prices).

> Is AI infrastructure being built faster than economically productive demand is growing?

Do not confuse physical demand for GPUs with economic demand for profitable AI services.

## 6. The AI deflation problem

AI models may become dramatically cheaper and more capable simultaneously. Falling AI prices do NOT automatically mean falling demand. Distinguish volume growth from economic value growth: if token consumption rises 500% while price per token falls 90%, revenue may not rise proportionally. Track token volume, price per token, revenue per token, compute cost per token, gross margin per token, revenue per GPU-hour, revenue per MW and revenue per data-center capacity unit.

## 7. Infrastructure economics

Build a dedicated infrastructure stress model. Track hyperscaler capex, AI-specific capex where identifiable, data-center construction, GPU depreciation, accelerator replacement cycles, electricity costs, power availability, financing costs, debt financing, lease obligations, utilization, compute pricing and expected useful life.

**Required revenue:** estimate the revenue required for deployed AI infrastructure to achieve break-even, 5%, 10%, 15% and 20% returns. These are scenario thresholds, not forecasts.

## 8. Capital flow

Track venture capital, private equity, IPOs, follow-on offerings, corporate capex, debt issuance, project finance, infrastructure financing, strategic investments, government subsidies and cloud credits. Compare inflows with revenue creation, free cash flow, economic output and productivity evidence.

> How much new capital is required to sustain each additional dollar of AI-related revenue?

## 9. Market structure

Do not treat "AI" as one asset class. Layers: (1) foundation models, (2) compute, (3) hyperscalers, (4) data centers, (5) AI software, (6) AI-exposed non-AI companies, (7) financial markets. Analyze each independently. A bubble may exist in one layer while another remains healthy.

## 10. Circular capital analysis

A major research priority. Company A invests in Company B; Company B buys compute or services from Company A or its ecosystem; Company B reports rising revenue; Company A raises its valuation partly on ecosystem growth. Identify potential circular financing, customer concentration, related-party economics, supplier financing, cloud credits, strategic investments and reciprocal commercial relationships. Do not automatically classify these as fraudulent or artificial.

> How much observed AI economic activity represents genuinely incremental external demand versus capital recycling within the AI ecosystem?

## 11. Customer concentration

Track top-customer and top-5 percentages, cloud-provider dependence, strategic-investor dependence, related-party revenue, contractual versus usage-based revenue, and long-term contracts versus short-term consumption. A company with enormous growth but extreme concentration should receive a different risk profile from one with diversified recurring demand.

## 12. Profitability quality

Do not simply record profit or loss. Decompose revenue minus COGS to gross profit, minus R&D, sales and marketing and G&A to operating income, and operating cash flow minus capex to free cash flow. Also examine stock-based compensation, depreciation, amortization, capitalized costs, fair-value adjustments, financing effects and non-cash items. Distinguish **accounting**, **economic** and **cash** profitability.

## 13. Market exuberance

Possible indicators: valuation expansion, IPO activity, issuance volume, retail participation, options activity, volatility, momentum, correlation, concentration, analyst target dispersion, short interest, insider selling and price/revenue expansion. Use historical comparisons (dot-com, 1929 where appropriate, Japanese asset bubble, housing/credit bubble, crypto cycles, previous semiconductor investment cycles). Do not mechanically claim equivalence with any bubble. The purpose is to identify similar statistical characteristics, not to force analogies.

## 14. Physical constraints

Track electricity, water, grid connection, transmission, land, cooling, semiconductor supply, HBM supply, networking equipment, transformers and construction timelines. Separate **constraint** from **externality**: a shortage of electricity can increase infrastructure value, while a social or environmental externality can increase regulatory and operating risk. They are not the same thing.

## 15. Productivity reality check

AI investment can be economically justified even if individual AI companies lose money. Measure labor productivity, software-development productivity, corporate adoption, revenue per employee, output per worker, automation, operating-cost reductions, time savings, new product creation and enterprise ROI.

> Is AI generating measurable economic productivity sufficient to justify the capital being deployed?

This is the strongest counterargument to a pure bubble thesis.

## 16. AI Bubble Stress Index

A composite index with a starting hypothesis of weights (not final; test alternatives): Valuation 25%, Capex/Monetization 20%, Supply-Demand Imbalance 20%, Profitability/Cash-Flow 15%, Market Exuberance 10%, Capital-Flow/Financing 5%, Physical Infrastructure 5%. Report the composite and the individual component scores. Never hide the underlying variables behind a single number.

## 17. Confidence score

Every output must include confidence, because data may be incomplete, delayed, estimated, privately reported, inconsistent across companies or affected by accounting differences. Never manufacture precision.

## 18. Regime detection

Eventually identify regimes: fundamental expansion, speculative expansion, fragility, bubble stress, deflation/reset and capitulation. These are analytical regimes, not predictions.

## 19. Bubble burst detection

Do NOT attempt to predict the date of a crash. Construct a break-risk framework from conditions that historically precede major repricing: falling revenue growth or gross margins, declining compute prices or utilization, excess capacity, accelerating capex, slowing adoption, rising financing costs, declining multiples, rising defaults, inventory accumulation, cancelled data-center projects, declining venture funding, IPO failures, insider selling and earnings misses.

> How many independent stress conditions are simultaneously present?

## 20. Falsification engine

**What would prove the bubble thesis wrong?** AI revenue consistently exceeds infrastructure investment requirements; AI gross margins improve materially; compute utilization stays high despite large capacity expansion; enterprise AI ROI becomes demonstrably strong; productivity growth accelerates; AI applications generate diversified recurring revenue; capex produces sufficiently high returns; valuations fall while fundamentals catch up; infrastructure becomes productive faster than expected. The engine should actively search for these signals.

## 21. Data integrity rules

Every metric carries metadata: source, publication date, observation period, geographic scope, methodology, units, actual/estimated/forecast status, revision history, confidence and limitations. Never mix actual with forecast data, or company-reported figures with third-party estimates, without clearly labeling them.

## 22. Source hierarchy

Prefer: (1) SEC filings, (2) company financial statements, (3) government statistics, (4) central banks, (5) regulatory filings, (6) exchange data, (7) energy agencies, (8) independent academic research, (9) reputable financial institutions, (10) high-quality financial journalism, (11) industry reports, (12) alternative datasets. Social media may be used for sentiment discovery but is not authoritative financial evidence.

## 23. Statistical methods

Investigate z-scores, percentile ranks, rolling averages and correlations, principal component analysis, factor models, regression, time-series analysis, structural-break detection, explosive-root tests (SADF, GSADF), volatility regimes, change-point detection, Granger causality where appropriate, cointegration, scenario analysis and sensitivity analysis. Every method needs an economic justification. Do not use sophisticated mathematics merely to look sophisticated.

## 24. Avoid double counting

NVIDIA revenue growth, GPU demand, data-center capex, AI capex and hyperscaler capex may represent overlapping economic activity. Do not count the same phenomenon several times and read the composite as independent evidence. Build a dependency map between variables.

## 25. Avoid false precision

Never output "AI bubble probability = 83.72%" unless a defensible, validated probabilistic model produces it. A score is not automatically a probability. Use **Stress Score**, not Probability of Crash.

## 26. Research workflow

For every major hypothesis: (1) define the hypothesis; (2) define evidence that would support it; (3) define evidence that would reject it; (4) find the best datasets; (5) test data quality; (6) construct the metric; (7) backtest against historical periods; (8) test alternative specifications; (9) run sensitivity analysis; (10) attempt to falsify the result; (11) only then incorporate it into the composite index.

## 27. Historical backtesting

Test whether the methodology would have detected stress before the dot-com bubble, the 2008 housing/credit bubble, the Japanese asset bubble, semiconductor investment cycles and crypto cycles.

> Would this methodology have detected excessive expectations before historical bubbles experienced major repricing?

If the methodology fails historical validation, modify or discard it.

## 28. Output dashboard

The final system displays the composite score, the component breakdown, fundamental-support indicators (revenue growth, adoption, productivity, gross margins, free cash flow), bubble-stress indicators (valuation stretch, capex/revenue, capital intensity, capacity growth, financing risk, market concentration), evidence against and for the bubble thesis, data confidence, historical percentile, regime, and key variables changing this month.

## 29. Research journal

Maintain a permanent journal. For every major change record the hypothesis, date, data used, methodology, result, conclusion, weaknesses, subsequent evidence and whether the hypothesis survived. Never silently change the methodology. Version the index (ABSI v0.1, v0.2, v0.3) so retrospective optimization is detectable.

## 30. Adversarial review

Before accepting any major conclusion ask: how could this conclusion be wrong? Construct the strongest counterargument. For every strong bubble signal find at least one plausible alternative explanation and test it quantitatively. Example: "AI capex is much larger than current AI revenue" may reflect infrastructure built ahead of demand because capacity takes years to construct.

## 31. No narrative substitution

"AI is revolutionary" and "AI is overhyped" are not metrics. Convert narratives into testable propositions. Example: "AI demand will eventually be enormous" becomes "AI compute utilization and monetized inference volume should increase sufficiently to produce X% annual growth in revenue per deployed MW."

## 32. The ultimate question

> Is the economic value being created by AI growing quickly enough, profitably enough and sustainably enough to justify the capital being committed to AI?

If yes, bubble stress should decline. If no, it should rise. If the evidence is ambiguous, the model should say so.

## 33. Development philosophy

Do not rush to build the dashboard. First build the research architecture and prove that the variables are meaningful, the datasets are reliable, the variables are sufficiently independent, the formulas make economic sense, the methodology survives historical testing, does not simply confirm the original hypothesis, and is robust under alternative assumptions. The objective is a defensible quantitative research instrument, not a visually impressive "AI bubble meter." If the original concept is flawed, change it. The integrity of the measurement system is more important than the original hypothesis.
